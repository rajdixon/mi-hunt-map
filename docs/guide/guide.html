  2. Enter your email. A one-time code is emailed to you; type it in. You stay signed in for a while, then it asks again.

Public land and map layers

Tap Layers (bottom right) to choose what shows.

Lands open to hunting

State ForestDNR state forest land
Game areasState game and wildlife areas
State parksOnly the parts open to hunting
National ForestHuron-Manistee, Hiawatha, Ottawa
Commercial ForestPrivate land open on foot for hunting
HAPPrivate land leased for public hunting; check-in rules apply

Land boundaries are stored on your phone, so they work with no signal. Tap any parcel for its name and details. Tap 3D (bottom left) to tilt the map and see the hills.

Backgrounds

Topo, Satellite, LiDAR terrain (bare-earth shading that shows ridges, benches, draws and creek beds through the trees), USGS Topo and Imagery + Topo.

Overlays

  • Forest stand cover type: aspen, oak, pine, swamp conifer and more (zoom in).
  • Logging on state land: recent cuts, active timber sales and planned cuts (zoom in).
  • DNR roads (with ORV status), DNR trails (including hunter walking trails), DNR parking.
  • Sections and townships, Deer management units.
  • Private land owners: owner names and acres (Kalamazoo County).
  • Possibly landlocked public land, Weather radar and Scent cones, each covered later in this guide.

Overlays load from the DNR as you move, so they need a signal.

Forest cover types

With Forest stand cover type on, a list of cover types opens under it, each with its map color: aspen & birch, oak, hardwoods, pine & upland conifer, swamp conifer, openings & brush, marsh & bog, water, and other.

  • Untick any type to hide it.
  • Only shows just that type. For example, tap Only next to Oak to see where the oak stands are.
  • All and None reset the list. Your choice is remembered.

Tap a stand for its cover type, size class, age and canopy.

Possibly landlocked public land

Turn it on in Layers. Red pieces are public land that no public road touches, not even through neighbouring State Forest, Commercial Forest or HAP land. Pieces like these often see less pressure.

Tap one for its name, acres and how you might get in.

Getting in legally: you may reach it by water, a DNR trail, a forest road, an easement, or with a landowner's permission to cross. Crossing private land without permission is trespassing.

Logging on state land

Three overlays in Layers, under Logging on state land. Zoom in to see them.

Recent cuts

State forest stands that started growing back in the last 10 years, usually after a harvest. They're shaded by age:

0–2 yearsFresh cut: slash, stumps, new sprouts
3–5 yearsThick regrowth, peak browse
6–10 yearsDense cover; good bedding

Active timber sales

Brown stripes. The DNR has sold or laid out these areas, so they're being logged or will be soon. Expect crews, trucks and new skid trails, and check before you hang a stand.

Planned cuts

Purple stripes with a dashed edge. These are harvests the DNR has proposed for coming years. Tap one for the kind of cut (clearcut, thinning, selection…), the earliest start, and the forester's own plan for the stand.

Good to know: this covers land the DNR manages (State Forest, game areas, and some National Forest sales it runs). Logging on Commercial Forest and HAP land is done by private owners and isn't shown. Planned cuts can change: the DNR takes public comments on each forest compartment before approving them.

3D view

Tap 3D (bottom left). The map tilts and the land rises, so ridges, benches, saddles and creek bottoms stand out. Land boundaries, pins, tracks and overlays all lie on the ground.

  • Tilt: drag two fingers up or down.
  • Turn: twist two fingers. The compass button appears above 3D; tap it to point north again.
  • On a computer: use the arrow buttons that appear above 3D (up and down tilt, the curved arrows turn), or hold the right mouse button and drag.
  • Tap 3D again to go back to a flat, north-up map.

Satellite in 3D is the best way to read a funnel or a bedding bench before you walk in.

3D hills in Layers sets how much the hills are raised: Low, Medium (the default) or High. Michigan's ground is gentle, so a little lift makes ridges and draws easier to read.

Good to know: 3D needs a signal for the elevation data and uses more battery, so it stays off until you turn it on. Saving an offline area while tilted saves what's around the middle of the screen, not out to the horizon.

Hunting hours and the day's outlook

The box at the top always shows legal hours for where you are (or the middle of the map) and the weather. Tap it for the full picture.

The hours box

  • Opens – closes, how long is left, and the county.
  • Temperature, wind (the arrow points where it's blowing), pressure and its trend, and the sky.
  • After legal light ends it switches to tomorrow: tomorrow's hours, the time until opening, and the forecast for first light.

"At a glance" (top of the panel)

  • Deer activity, 1 to 5: Great, Good, Fair or Slow day, with the reasons. The rut, a cold snap, a pressure change and your stands getting their wind count for it; strong wind, likely rain and heat count against.
  • Right now: temperature, sky, wind, pressure, rain chance.
  • Outlook: high and low, cold snap flag, what the pressure does, and conditions at first light and in the last hour.
  • Deer activity by hour: bars for each hour of legal light, tallest around first and last light, higher at midday in the rut, lower in windy, wet or warm hours. Best lists the top stretches; a green dot means one of your stands has its wind that hour.
  • Your stands nearby: the times each stand's wind is right, and what your log says works there.
  • Rut: where the rut usually is on that date in Michigan (pre-rut, seeking, chasing, peak breeding, post-rut, second rut) and what it means.
  • Moon phase, and which deer seasons are open.

Below that

  • Next 7 days: tap a day to plan it. The five small bars are that day's activity score. ▼8° marks a cold snap; arrows show pressure (↓↑ is a front passing).
  • More detail: the hours table, forecast wording, what the pressure means for deer, moon times, solunar periods, and the rules: seasons, antler restrictions, antlerless licenses, baiting.
It learns from you: once your pins have 8 sits logged (log "No deer" too), the forecast leans toward the temperatures, pressure and time of day you've actually seen deer in. The moon is left out: GPS-collar studies found it makes little difference, while the rut and temperature drops matter most.

Pins and wind

Drop a pin

  • Press and hold on the map (right-click on a computer), or open My pins and tap Pin my location or Pin map center.
  • Pick a type: tree stand, ground blind, camp, trail cam, buck rub, buck scrape, parking or other.
  • Add a name and notes (access route, shooting lanes).

Good winds

For stands and blinds, tick the winds the spot hunts well in (where the wind comes from). The pin then shows:

  • Whether right now is a good wind, and the good-wind stretches in legal hours over the next days.
  • A Good wind / Wrong wind badge in your pin list.
  • Its good-wind times in the day's outlook, and in an evening notification for tomorrow.

My pins lists everything by distance from you, with the wind card for your area, your lines and areas, and backup buttons.

Hunt log and your patterns

Open a pin and tap Add entry after each sit:

  • Sighting, Shot, Harvest, No deer or Note, plus what happened.
  • The time and the weather (wind, temperature, pressure) fill in by themselves.

After 3 sits at a spot, Your patterns shows what worked: sits with deer out of sits logged, by wind, morning or evening, temperature and pressure trend. The best one is in green.

Log the empty sits too. Tap No deer when nothing shows, or every sit looks like a good one.

Trail cam photos

  • Open a pin (usually a trail cam) and tap Add photos. Pick from your camera roll or Files; a whole card's worth at once is fine.
  • Each photo shows the time the camera took it, not when you uploaded it.
  • Your phone shrinks photos before uploading, so a big trail cam picture becomes about half a megabyte.
  • On a trail cam pin, adding photos logs a Cam check; the pin list shows "Checked 3 days ago · 24 photos".
  • Tap a photo for full screen: add a note ("8-point, from the swamp"), step through newer and older (arrow keys on a computer), or delete.

Uploading needs a signal. A photo you've opened once is kept for offline viewing.

Scent cones

Turn on Scent cones in Layers. Each tree stand and ground blind gets a wedge showing where your scent drifts for this hour's forecast wind, about 300 m downwind. In calm air it's a circle around the stand.

The cone turns red when it covers one of your trail cams, rubs or scrapes, and the pin list warns you ("Scent over Rub line now").

Go to

Open any pin (yours or a partner's) and tap Go to. You get:

  • A big arrow pointing the way, the distance, compass heading and walking time.
  • A dashed line on the map from you to it.
  • A buzz when you're within about 15 yards.

The first time, your iPhone asks to use the compass. Allow it, or tap Compass later. Until it has the compass, the arrow points against map north ("arrow is map-up"); once you're walking it uses your direction of travel.

Works with no signal. GPS and the compass don't need cell service. Tap Stop when you're there.

Tracks

Tap Tracks (right side), then Start recording as you walk in. The orange line follows you, the green dot is where you started, and a dotted line points straight back to it with the distance.

Stop and save when you're done. Saved tracks can be shown or hidden on the map, saved as GPX, shared with partners, or deleted.

Keep the app open on screen while recording. Phones stop GPS for web apps when the screen locks or you switch apps; recording picks up again when you come back. Turn on Keep the screen on in Settings for long walks.

Lines and areas

  1. Tap the pencil at the top.
  2. Pick Line (property line, route in, drag line, shooting lane) or Area (food plot, bedding area, sanctuary, thicket).
  3. Tap points on the map. The length or acres show as you go; Undo removes the last point.
  4. Tap Done, then name it (or tap a suggestion), pick a color, add notes, and Save.

Tap a line or area later to rename it, Change points, or delete it. They're listed under My pins.

Measure and safety zones

Tap the ruler at the top.

  • Distance / area: tap points for the distance; three or more points also give the acres inside. Tap a pin to measure to it exactly.
  • 450 ft ring: tap a house or farm building to draw Michigan's safety zone. No hunting inside it, gun or bow, without written permission from the owner or occupant.

Weather radar

Turn on Weather radar in Layers to see live rain from the National Weather Service, updated every 5 minutes. The bar at the bottom shows the time of the latest scan.

Tap Play last hour to loop the last 50 minutes and see which way the rain is moving. Tap × to turn radar off. Radar needs a signal.

Offline maps and search

Offline maps

Land boundaries, your pins, the hours and the moon always work offline. The background map needs saving first:

  1. With a signal, move the map over where you'll hunt.
  2. Tap Offline maps (the down arrow), check the size, and tap Save this area.

Save each background you want (Topo, Satellite, LiDAR) separately. Saved areas show as dashed outlines.

Search

Tap the magnifier and search for a game area, town, your pins, a county, a DMU, a township and section (T29N R2W S23) or coordinates.

Hunting partners

If you're part of a hunting group, you can share the spots you choose, with the whole group or only certain people, and see each other on the map. Nothing is shared unless you tick it.

Share a pin, line or area

  1. Open it and tick Share with partners.
  2. Choose who: Everyone in the group (including anyone who joins later), or Only the people I pick and tick their names.
  3. That shares the spot, name and type. Tick anything else you want them to see: Notes, Good winds, Hunt log, Photos. Each is off until you turn it on.

Tracks have a Share… button in the Tracks list with the same choices. Your lists show who each thing is shared with. Untick something, or untick a person, and it disappears from their map.

What partners see

  • Only what you shared with them, with your initial on it. Lines, areas and tracks show dashed. They can look, and use Go to, but can't change anything.
  • Only the extras you ticked. They never see who else you shared something with.
  • They get a notification when you share something new with them.

Where everyone is

In My pins, under Hunting partners, tick Share my location, then choose everyone or only certain people. They see you as a blue dot with your initial and "seen 3 min ago", and the list shows how far away and which direction each partner is, with Show and Go to.

Your phone only sends your location while the app is open on screen. With the phone locked or another app in front, partners see your last spot and how long ago it was. Set the name partners see you as in the same place.

To join a group, ask the group owner to add your email.

Notifications

The bell at the top shows how many notifications you haven't seen. Tap it for the list; tap one with a place to fly the map there.

  • A partner shared something with you.
  • Good wind tomorrow: each evening, the times tomorrow when your stands' good winds line up with legal hours.
  • Cold front: each evening, when tomorrow's high is 8°F or more below today's.

Under Notify me when, untick any kind you don't want.

Alerts on your phone

Tap Turn on alerts in the bell panel and allow notifications, once on each phone or computer. You then get an alert even when the app is closed. Send a test checks it works.

iPhone: alerts only work in the app opened from your Home Screen (see Getting started), not in Safari.

Sync, backups and good to know

Your data

  • Pins, hunt logs, photos, tracks, lines and areas are saved on your phone and on the private server. Changes sync whenever you have a signal; the top of My pins says when it last synced.
  • Sign in on another phone, tablet or computer and everything is there.
  • The server is backed up every night. You can also save your own copy: My pins, then Full backup. Import brings a backup or GPX file back in, and Pins as GPX opens in onX, HuntStand, Gaia and Google Earth.

Good to know

  • GPS only runs while the app is open on screen. This is true for every web app on a phone. On long sits, turn on Battery saver in Settings.
  • Weather, radar, overlays, notifications and photo uploads need a signal. Land boundaries, pins, hours, the moon, Go to and saved offline maps don't.
  • If the app says you're signed out, reload it and enter a new email code.
  • When an update comes out, the app reloads itself once. Settings, then Check for update, does it right away. This guide is updated with each new feature.
This map is a guide, not a legal boundary. It doesn't show safety zones around buildings (use the 450 ft ring), posted closures, refuges inside open areas, or local ordinances. The hours and rules summarise the 2026 Michigan Deer Hunting Digest. Always check the full digest and Michigan.gov/DNRLaws before you hunt.
